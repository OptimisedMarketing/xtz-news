<h2>From a digital-art marketplace to a physical pack</h2>

<p>objkt describes itself as a Tezos marketplace where artists and collectors create, sell, discover and collect digital art. Kento extends that collecting context into a physical format. The Kento campaign calls its offering booster packs of physical art cards by digital artists from around the world, while the project’s public channels describe the idea as a way of giving digital art another form in people’s hands.</p>

<p>The campaign’s public materials say each card can connect to the original digital artwork through a QR and scratch-off claim system. That link is an important distinction: Kento is not describing a conventional trading-card game. It is presenting physical cards as an entry point for discovering and collecting artist work, with a digital claim layer built on objkt infrastructure.</p>

<h2>Web Nostalgia: 117 cards, 40 artists, seven rarities</h2>

<p>Kento’s official site says Season 01 is built around <strong>Web Nostalgia</strong> and lists 117 physical cards from 40 artists across seven rarities: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Ultra Rare, Secret Rare, Grail and Promo. The catalogue includes work by artists such as Amber Vittoria, Anna Lucia, Canek Zapata, Ganbrood, qubibi, Riniifish and Zancan.</p>

<p>That roster gives the collection a broad artist-led frame rather than a single-brand character set. The cards shown in Kento’s launch materials lean into early-web references, interfaces, glitches, archives and personal digital memory—the visual territory the project calls Web Nostalgia.</p>

<h2>What the campaign milestone does—and does not—mean</h2>

<p>The featured image supplied to XTZ.News marks a campaign milestone of more than $100,000 raised in 24 hours. It is a snapshot of the campaign’s early momentum, not a substitute for the live Kickstarter total, which may be different when readers visit the page.</p>

<p>Like any crowdfunding project, Kento remains subject to its published Kickstarter conditions, including campaign funding, production and fulfilment. The campaign says its cards are prototyped and manufacturing is confirmed, while also acknowledging that physical production can involve delays or quality variations. Listed rewards currently show estimated delivery in December 2026.</p>

<h2>A new collecting doorway for Tezos art</h2>

<p>Kento’s relevance to the Tezos art ecosystem is not that a physical card automatically guarantees a particular market outcome. It is that the team behind a long-running Tezos digital-art marketplace is testing a physical route into artist discovery and collection. For people already following Tezos art, the project creates a new format to watch; for people encountering the work through a booster pack, it offers a possible first path back to the artists and their digital practice.</p>

<p>Readers considering a pledge should review the current Kickstarter page, including rewards, risks, delivery estimates and campaign conditions, directly before making a decision.</p>
