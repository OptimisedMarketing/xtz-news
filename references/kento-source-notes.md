# Kento Art-Collecting Story — Source Notes

**Research date:** 7 September 2026

## Confirmed facts for publication

Kento is presented by its public channels as a collection of physical art cards built around original works by digital artists. Its official site says **Season 01: Web Nostalgia** is live on Kickstarter and describes a set of **117 physical cards**, **40 artists**, and **7 rarities**, with worldwide shipping advertised.[1]

Kento’s Kickstarter campaign is run by **objkt AG** in the Digital Art category. At the time of review, the campaign page showed **£95,819 pledged** toward a **£4,022 goal**, with **597 backers** and **23 days remaining**. These live metrics must be attributed to the time of publication because Kickstarter totals and remaining time change. The page states that the campaign is all-or-nothing and gives an estimated delivery date of December 2026 for listed reward tiers.[2]

The campaign describes booster packs filled with physical art cards by digital artists. Its risk section says cards are prototyped and manufacturing is confirmed, while noting that physical production can still bring delays or quality variations. It also states that the QR and scratch-off claim system is built on objkt’s existing infrastructure.[2]

objkt identifies itself as a Tezos digital-art marketplace and describes Kento as a project from the same team. The objkt site included a direct **Back Kento** call to action during review.[3]

The supplied Kento X profile describes the project as “a new way to collect art,” live on Kickstarter, and from the team behind objkt. The supplied Instagram material says the first season explores Web Nostalgia; its official reel describes booster packs featuring original work from a roster of 37 artists in the launch announcement, while the currently published official Kento site lists a 40-artist Season 01 roster. The article should therefore use the official site’s current 40-artist count and avoid conflating it with the earlier announcement roster.[4] [5]

## Editorial framing

The story should treat Kento as an active crowdfunding campaign, not a finished product or guaranteed delivery. It should distinguish physical cards from any QR/scratch-off connection to artwork and should not imply investment value, resale outcomes, or guaranteed reward delivery.
